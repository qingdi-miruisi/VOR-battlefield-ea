Dear Editor,

On behalf of the author, I am pleased to submit the enclosed manuscript
entitled \emph{``Battlefield-Adaptive Evolutionary Algorithm with Online
Reference-Reallocation and Quantile $\varepsilon$-Grid Selection for
Large-Scale and Constrained Multi-Objective Optimization''} for
consideration for publication in \emph{Applied Soft Computing}.

This paper proposes \textsc{VOR}, a dimension-adaptive evolutionary
algorithm whose central contribution is a \emph{battlefield routing} rule
that services the large-scale ($D=300$) and the constrained
low-dimensional ($D=10$--$60$) battlefields from a \emph{single
codebase}. On the six-problem benchmark with the four traditional
baselines and our \textsc{EDD} lineage, \textsc{VOR} attains the first
IGD rank on three of six problems at 30 seeds, with a statistically
significant advantage over \textsc{EDD} on \textsc{ZDT1},
\textsc{LSMOP1} and \textsc{CF1} (30-seed Wilcoxon, $p<10^{-5}$).
Very importantly, we position the result honestly: against the 2026
SOTA trio (\textsc{FDSEA}/\textsc{GDVTSF}/\textsc{MOEA-IB}, the same
30-seed protocol), \textsc{FDSEA} outperforms \textsc{VOR} on all three
$D=300$ instances; \textsc{VOR}'s differentiating value is the
\emph{single-codebase coverage} of the constrained low-dimensional
battlefield where the three SOTA algorithms have no published
$D=10$--$60$ benchmark.

We believe this manuscript fits \emph{Applied Soft Computing} well for
three reasons. First, the contribution is a \emph{practical} one: a
single codebase that removes the need to re-implement a dimension
adaptive algorithm for a new problem class. Second, the experimental
protocol is statistically sound (30 seeds, Friedman and Wilcoxon
significance tests, 5-algorithm $+$ 3-SOTA comparison set). Third, the
honest-boundary reporting (the \textsc{EDD}-family seed-invariance on
\textsc{MaF14}, the \textsc{DTLZ2}-300D \textsc{EDD} margin, the
\textsc{CF1} decomposition structural advantage) matches the journal's
emphasis on reproducible, well-characterized application research rather
than a single claimed champion algorithm.
